# 17: A drop-mode switch on every category, and new inventory categories

Status: ready-for-agent

**Recommended model:** `opus`: settings move from old keys to new ones, and a mistake would silently lose players' or packs' saved choices.

**Blocked by:** None (can start immediately)

**What to build:** Replace the two drop-mode exemption settings with one "Include in drop mode" switch on each category rule, and add four inventory categories: Raw materials, Knowledge, Genetics and Body parts. Classification must follow the pack survey in [pack-item-categories.md](../pack-item-categories.md) (1,559 downloaded Workshop mods and the DLCs, 2026-11-24), which names the def names, categories and classes to detect. Source of truth: the spec's "Which categories the drop mode judges" and "Raw materials" paragraphs, and the decision rows "Drop mode scope", "Raw materials category", "Materials and eras" and "Scrap amount for stacks" (all settled 2026-11-24). Do not redesign them. The era-less stuff rule in `EraReader.ItemEra` and the per-stack scrap rounding in `ItemClassifier.ScrapUnits` / `ScrapAmounts.StackUnits` are already in the working tree; build on them.

## Scope

**Rule field and evaluator**

- Defaults: `inDropMode` true for weapons and apparel, false for ammunition, drugs, medicine, food, rawMaterials and other. Set in the field initialisers, `Source/Model/Presets.cs` (both places that set the old fields, lines ~91 and ~130; Progression, Cleanup, Vanilla all the same), and `Defs/SettingsDefaults.xml`. The test that the shipped XML equals the compiled Progression preset must pass.
- `Source/Model/LootEvaluator.cs` `ApplyDropMode`: replace the two category checks with `if (!(rules.RuleFor(item.Category)?.inDropMode ?? false)) return null;` before the mode switch, for every drop mode. A weapon or apparel carried in the inventory counts as gear: `InventoryCategoryOf` must classify by `EraReader.IsGear(def)` first and return Weapon or Apparel (check how `GearSorter` and `ItemClassifier` classify worn and carried gear today and make carried gear take the gear categories, so its switch is the weapon or apparel one).

**New categories and classification**
- `Source/Model/DropCandidate.cs` `ItemCategory`: add `Knowledge`, `Genetics`, `BodyParts`, `RawMaterials`. `Source/Model/ItemCategories.cs`: add the rows (fields `knowledge`, `genetics`, `bodyParts`, `rawMaterials`; label keys `LootScavenging_Category<Name>`), before Other in page order. Each defaults to Keep with its switch off. Lock names and saved keys come from the table.
- **XML exception list.** Add a def (for example `LootScavenging.CategoryOverridesDef`, one shipped instance in `Defs/CategoryOverrides.xml`) with two lists. The first maps item def names to an inventory category; the second maps thing category def names to one, matching the category or any of its parents. It ships with the survey's stragglers (the two Medieval Overhaul bolts and `ReelTurretAmmo` as Ammunition) and any other named case the implementation needs. Entries are plain strings matched at classification time, so an entry for an item or category that isn't loaded does nothing and logs nothing; no `MayRequire` needed. Packs and players extend it with an XML patch. It can name only inventory categories: it can't make an item gear, and gear is judged first. Item entries beat category entries, and both beat the checks below. Build a lookup once after defs load, so classification stays a dictionary lookup. Document the def in the header comment of the XML file with one example patch, and add a short line to the spec's raw materials section.
- `Source/Loot/ItemClassifier.InventoryCategoryOf` check order, first match wins (the XML list applies right after gear):
  1. Gear: `EraReader.IsGear(def)` gives Weapon or Apparel. Never by category ancestry: Combat Extended files its ammo under `Weapons`.
  2. Ammunition: a category named "Ammo" (as now), or the `MortarShells` tree, or the reloadable ammo of a weapon or turret where the game exposes it. The survey's stragglers must land here: Medieval Overhaul `DankPyon_ScorpioBolt` and `DankPyon_BallistaBolt`, and Reel's Turret Pipeline `ReelTurretAmmo`. If no general signal catches them, they go in the XML exception list below, never in C#.
  3. Medicine (`IsMedicine`, as now).
  4. Drugs: `IsDrug`, or anything under the `Drugs` category tree. This catches the 13 serums and catalysts with no drug comp, such as `MechSerumResurrector`, `DeathrestCapacitySerum`, the Anomaly serums and `SentienceCatalyst`.
  5. Food (as now).
  6. Knowledge: a `Book` thing class or book comp; the `Books`, `Techprints` or `Neurotrainers` trees; Research Papers' `ResearchPaper`. Techprints and neurotrainers are generated at load, so check them in game.
  7. Genetics: the `Genepack`, `Xenogerm`, `HumanEmbryo` or `HumanOvum` classes or defs; Vanilla Genetics Expanded's `GR_*` trees and Alpha Genes' `AG_GeneTools`, `AG_Alphapack` and `AG_Mixedpack`.
  8. Body parts: the `BodyParts` tree.
  9. Usable items to Other: a usable or use-effect comp, a studiable comp, or a thing class other than `Thing` or `ThingWithComps`. These are loot boxes, weapon kits, quest cards and Anomaly `Shard`, so they never count as raw materials.
  10. Raw materials: `def.IsStuff`, or the `ResourcesRaw` or `Manufactured` trees.
  11. Other.
- Verified in the game's Core defs (`Defs/ThingCategoryDefs/ThingCategories.xml`): `ResourcesRaw` has children `PlantMatter` and `StoneBlocks` only. Components, chemfuel and neutroamine are filed under `Manufactured`, not `ResourcesRaw`, and are not stuff, yet the spec lists them as raw materials. Resolve it by also matching the `Manufactured` category tree (excluding anything already caught by the earlier checks, and excluding `MortarShells` and its children, which are ammunition-like). Dump the matched defs for Core (and Royalty, Ideology, Biotech, Anomaly if installed) once while building, confirm components, chemfuel, neutroamine, steel, plasteel, wood, stone blocks, silver, gold, devilstrand and cloth land in Raw materials, and note any surprise in the ticket comments. Also confirm a serum, a mortar shell, a techprint, a genepack, a bionic arm and a loot box each land in their category. Note in the spec any case where the implementation differs from its wording.
- Check that `Scrap everything` with the switch on turns a no-era stack into scrap of the colony's era (`ItemEra` returns an era-less result for stuff; the scrap era for no-era items must resolve to the colony era, not fail or use the fallback setting). Fix if not.

**Classification drift check (developer only, no player effect)**
Mod authors can refile items between categories in an update. This check makes that show up as a failed case instead of a player report. It must not change anything a player sees or runs: no setting, no startup work, no saved data, no log output outside dev mode.
- A `[DebugAction]` in `Source/Loot/InventoryDebugActions.cs` (dev mode only, like the actions there) that classifies every loaded item def with `InventoryCategoryOf`'s rules and writes counts per category, plus each def per category, to a file under the game's config folder or the log. It runs only when clicked.

**Review findings to fold in (review of 2026-11-24, `work/reviews/2026-11-24-mod-review.md` §6.1, 1.3, 3.1, 4.2)**
- **"Scrap everything" must not change meaning for existing configs.** Today it scraps every inventory item except medicine and ammunition. After this ticket a saved config with `dropMode=ScrapEverything` and none of the new keys would load with every inventory switch off and start dropping food, drugs, silver and the rest whole. In the settings move: when a loaded file has `ScrapEverything` and no `<category>InDropMode` key, switch on Food, Drugs, Other, Raw materials, Knowledge, Genetics and Body parts, and Medicine and Ammunition only if their old key was true. Unit-test it and say so in the 0.6.0 changelog. Packs are unaffected (their baseline is the XML).
- **Keep-original and non-gear switches.** In "Keep the original item" an out-of-reach item of a switched-on non-gear category would return Keep before its rule and can't carry the unfamiliar label (only gear can). In that mode, non-gear categories fall through to their category rule whatever the switch says; the switch row says so (or is disabled) in that mode.
- **The "Only gear beyond the colony's reach" condition** (`LootScavenging_OnlyBeyondReach`, `SettingsPage.cs` ~247) can't match for a category the drop mode handles in "Scrap out-of-reach gear" or "Keep the original item": out-of-reach items never reach the conditions step. With the switch, it is live exactly when the category's switch is off (or the drop mode is Off). Disable it with a one-line note when the switch is on in those two modes. Rename the label to "Only items beyond the colony's reach", since it shows under food, drugs and medicine too.
- **Strings this ticket must also rewrite** (and translate):
  - `LootScavenging_ItemsNoteScrapEverything` ("turns all gear into scrap, so these rules are not used"): wrong for every switched-off category.
  - The three `LootScavenging_DropMode*Explain` texts (~lines 132 to 134) and the Progression preset sentence: they say "gear" without the switch.
  - Labels and section texts for Knowledge, Genetics and Body parts, not just `LootScavenging_CategoryRawMaterials`. Add glossary rows first.
- **Stack scrap amounts: decided 2026-11-24 (Kyle), done in 0.5.2.** Scrap is never worth more than the item or stack it came from. Build on the 0.5.2 `ScrapAmounts` rule; the raw-materials switch adds no new risk.
- **Carried gear judged twice:** settled in 0.5.2. Gear kept at a downing under "Everything stays on the body" is skipped later (`BodyLoot.WasJudgedAtDowning`), so classifying carried gear as Weapon or Apparel no longer makes a second pass apply the weapon rule twice. Also fix `InventoryCategoryOf`'s doc comment, which says the opposite of the code.

Out of scope: any change to the era logic, scrap amounts (settled in 0.5.2), and new categories beyond the four above.
